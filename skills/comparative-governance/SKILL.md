# Skill: Comparative Governance

**Name:** `comparative-governance`  
**Status:** Draft 0.1  
**Purpose:** Produce a contextual, evidence-linked comparison of governance arrangements without assuming that one tradition or institutional label is universal.

## Inputs

- A comparative question, decision context, and intended reader.
- Cases, regions, or institutional arrangements to compare; if unspecified, propose a balanced sample and explain selection limits.
- Time period, available sources, and any access, language, or jurisdiction constraints.

## Procedure

1. Define the unit of analysis, relevant scale, time span, and whether the task is descriptive, causal, normative, or design-oriented.
2. For each case, identify primary rules and practices: participants and representation; decision rights; authority constraints; information and resources; enforcement; conflict resolution and appeal; exit, voice, and rule revision.
3. Situate each case in historical, cultural, ecological, economic, legal, and power context. Identify whose account is represented and whose is absent.
4. Gather primary material and credible scholarship. Record provenance, date/version, access limits, methods, and limitations using `templates/source-record.md`.
5. Search for counterevidence, critiques, failed cases, internal variation, and evidence from affected groups. Distinguish formal design from implementation.
6. Compare mechanisms and conditions, not labels alone. Preserve case-specific differences; avoid universal rankings or causal claims not supported by the design and evidence.
7. State findings with uncertainty, alternative interpretations, distributional effects, and evidence gaps. Separate documented facts, empirical findings, interpretation, normative premises, and proposals.

## Quality checks

- Every material factual claim has a traceable source or is explicitly marked unverified.
- Cases are not selected solely because they support a preferred conclusion; exclusions and selection effects are stated.
- Primary sources and at least one credible critical or independent perspective are considered where available.
- Categories and translations are not treated as culturally neutral by default.
- Outcomes are not attributed causally without suitable evidence; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.
- The work follows root `RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md` and is dated.

## Failure modes

Do not treat a constitution or institutional label as proof of actual practice; infer consent from participation rates alone; use one dataset as a complete history of governance; collapse distinct traditions into a Western/non-Western binary; or declare a universally best model from a small number of cases.

## Output

A concise synthesis with question and scope, case-selection rationale, comparison dimensions, findings by mechanism, counterevidence, distributional and power analysis, uncertainty, sources, and next research questions. Do not issue a policy prescription unless the normative premises and context are explicit.
