# Skill: AI Governance Review

**Name:** `ai-governance-review`  
**Status:** Draft 0.1  
**Purpose:** Review an AI governance proposal by tracing real actors, authority, controls, affected parties, and remedies—not only its principles or paperwork.

## Inputs

- Proposal, policy, product, procurement, or workflow being assessed.
- Jurisdictions, sector, lifecycle stage, intended users, and affected people.
- Available system, process, legal, evaluation, and incident documentation.

## Procedure

1. State the question and as-of date. Identify authoritative current legal/standards sources needed; do not infer legal compliance from a general framework.
2. Map the lifecycle and actors: developers, providers, deployers, procurers, operators, data subjects, workers, affected people, oversight bodies, and accountable decision owners.
3. Describe intended purpose, actual use, decision rights, data flows, model/tool dependencies, external effects, and foreseeable misuse or repurposing.
4. Trace obligations into controls: who can authorize, monitor, contest, stop, audit, and remedy; what evidence is retained; who handles incidents; what happens when controls fail.
5. Assess participation, rights, accessibility, power, privacy, security, labor, distributional impacts, environmental impacts, and context-specific risks. Seek perspectives of people likely to bear the harms.
6. Distinguish written commitments from operational enforcement and measured results. Inspect tests, audit scope/independence, incident records, and unresolved gaps where available.
7. Offer proportionate recommendations with owners, verification criteria, residual risks, review dates, and an escalation path. Label proposals as proposals, not proven mitigations.

## Quality checks

- Current legal and standards claims cite authoritative dated sources and specify jurisdiction/status.
- Every important safeguard is linked to an owner, mechanism, evidence artifact, and remedy or escalation where applicable.
- The analysis identifies conflicts of interest and independent-review limitations.
- Uncertainty, counterarguments, affected-party perspectives, and unassessed areas are visible.
- No claim of safety, fairness, compliance, or effectiveness is inferred from a checklist, model score, or framework adoption alone.
- Follow root `RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`; use `templates/publication-review.md` before external release.

## Failure modes

Do not conflate voluntary guidance with binding law; rely exclusively on vendor self-attestation; treat human oversight as meaningful without authority, time, competence, and ability to intervene; or mistake logging for accountability and remedy.

## Output

A dated review with scope, system and actor map, obligations-to-controls matrix, evidence and gaps, affected-party analysis, prioritized recommendations, residual risks, and source list. If the record is insufficient, state what cannot be concluded.
