# Agora Research Map

**Initial map · 2026-10-28**

This is a learning agenda, not a claim of exhaustive coverage. Each stream becomes a research program with explicit questions, reading list, methods, and outputs. Start with mechanisms and context, then synthesize across traditions.

## A. Human governance across time and cultures

**Questions:** What varied arrangements have people used to make rules, allocate authority, manage common resources, resolve disputes, represent groups, and constrain rulers? How did these work in practice and for whom? What are the limits of surviving records and comparative categories?

**Coverage:** kinship and customary institutions; councils and assemblies; city-states and empires; religious and legal orders; republics, monarchies, democracies, and federations; bureaucracies; labor and cooperative governance; commons and polycentric arrangements; colonial and postcolonial orders; contemporary community, platform, and transnational governance.

**Guardrail:** Do not use a list of regime labels as a history. Include primary sources, context, affected people's accounts where available, and critical historiography. Avoid treating any single dataset or canon as a complete map of human experience.

## B. Institutional design and collective action

**Questions:** Which mechanisms support cooperation, accountability, learning, representation, conflict resolution, and adaptation under different conditions? How do power and incentives alter formal rules? How are these effects evaluated?

## C. AI and human–AI governance

**Questions:** Who is accountable across model providers, deployers, procurers, operators, and affected people? How can participation, rights, oversight, incident response, audit, contestability, and remedy be made effective? Which controls work at the actual integration boundary?

**Topics:** public-sector AI; organizational accountability; standards and risk management; data governance; human oversight; safety/security; labor impacts; international coordination; assurance and evaluation; institutional capacity.

## D. Delegation, handoffs, and agentic work

**Questions:** How should a human delegate bounded work to agents and automations? What should an authorization specify—principal, task, scope, permitted tools/actions, limits, expiry, evidence, escalation, revocation, subdelegation, and responsibility? How do we test enforcement and actual revocation, including in-flight actions?

**Initial posture:** deterministic, least-privilege controls and clear approvals first; model-based authority changes are research hypotheses, not default controls.

## E. Technology, institutional capacity, and flourishing

**Questions:** When do digital systems and LLMs improve access, coordination, learning, deliberation, public services, or organizational capacity? Who benefits, who bears costs, what gets measured, and what becomes harder to contest?

**Measures:** context-specific dashboards that can include material outcomes, health, agency/capabilities, distribution, social connection, participation, trust, sustainability, quality and timeliness, and burdens. Avoid a single proxy treated as the whole of flourishing.

## F. Research infrastructure and publication

Develop reusable source registries, comparative case records, transparent synthesis, evaluation protocols, and accessible outputs. Preserve provenance and versioning; do not scrape or republish restricted databases or copyrighted source collections without permission.

## Initial outputs

1. A comparative map of governance mechanisms, traditions, and evidence gaps.
2. A human–AI delegation and handoff design brief with enforceability tests.
3. A framework for evaluating technology-enabled governance against human-centered outcomes and explicit baselines.

Each output must earn its claims under `RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md` and the publication gate. No target date implies completion or validation.
