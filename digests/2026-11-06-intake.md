## Records

### 1. Deterministic incident-response lifecycles do not transfer to agentic systems
Source: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8621/final · Published: 2026-11-02 · Checked: 2026-11-06 · Grade: A · Maturity: documented
Claim: A 400-participant NIST/industry workshop concluded that conventional, deterministic incident-response lifecycles (CSF 2.0, SP 800-61r3) break down against probabilistic, autonomous, deeply integrated agent systems, and named five operational needs: reasoning-layer telemetry, non-human identity governance, **authorization-scoped containment**, clear evidentiary and reporting schemas, and coordinated responsibility across distributed value chains.
Why it matters here: It moves the unit of revocation from "the agent" to "the authorization" — containment is scoped to what an agent was permitted to do, not to a process you kill — which is exactly the distinction Agora's experiment 001 rubric blurs at T3/T4.
Against: A synthesis of workshop perspectives, not an evaluation; no measured outcomes, no failures reported, and NIST itself frames future guidance as unbuilt. Not searched: independent critique of IR 8621 (published four days ago).
Verdict: **test** — amend the exp 001 rubric so T3 is scored as "halt the *authorization*, then the process" and T4 becomes conditional (some agentic effects are enumerated rather than reversible), and add reasoning-layer telemetry as the evidence type T1/T2 may draw on.
Artefact: issue to open — *exp 001: authorization-scoped containment and conditional T4* (after review)
Revisit: 2027-02-06, or when NIST publishes the SP 800-61 AI update it announces here

### 2. NIST proposes ten design tenets for agent identity — delegation attenuates, privilege never stands
Source: https://www.nist.gov/blogs/cybersecurity-insights/design-tenets-agent-identity-management-systems · Published: 2026-10-27 · Checked: 2026-11-06 · Grade: B · Maturity: proposal
Claim: NIST CAISSI/MITRE set out ten pre-decisional design tenets for agent IAM, of which four bear directly on delegated authority: delegated scopes **attenuate** so a downstream agent can never claim more authority than its parent (02); agents hold zero standing privilege (03); all grants are just-in-time, time-bounded and revocable, with no long-lived bearer tokens (04); and the evidence chain is append-only and architecturally independent of the agent (10).
Why it matters here: Agora's current control candidates measure *what the agent did*; tenet 02 supplies the missing structural property — authority is a chain of narrowing signed mandates, so revocation and audit are questions about the chain, not the run.
Against: Blog-stage and explicitly "initial"; no implementation evidence, no evaluation, and the final SP 1800 practice guide is future work. Internal tension unresolved: tenet 04 invokes continuous risk-based runtime verification while tenet 08 insists access decisions stay deterministic — the document does not say how a probabilistic risk signal may trigger revocation without becoming the decision-maker. That tension is itself the interesting research question.
Verdict: **adopt** (as vocabulary and control-set candidate, not as validated guidance) — add C4 *delegation-chain/attenuation* to exp 001's candidate control sets and cite the tenets as its source.
Artefact: issue to open — *exp 001: candidate C4, delegation chain with attenuation*; plus a `SOURCES.md` entry
Revisit: 2027-02-06, or on publication of the NCCoE SP 1800 practice guide

### 3. Offline-verifiable audit has a stated ceiling: the signer at signing time
Source: https://github.com/keelapi/keel-verifier · Published: 2026-10-31 (catalogue addition `#187`) · Checked: 2026-11-06 · Grade: B · Maturity: implemented
Claim: A MIT-licensed verifier checks exported governance evidence — decisions, dispatches, provider responses, closure records — for post-signing tampering, entirely offline, and states in its own README that pre-signing manipulation by a privileged operator "is NOT detected … requires hardware-backed signing (TEE/HSM)".
Why it matters here: It gives Agora a concrete, inspectable instance of the boundary that makes "auditable" a claim about *a threat model* rather than a property. Any control set Agora endorses must name who could have tampered before the signature.
Against: Vendor-authored tool and vendor-authored limitation; I inspected the README and install path only, not the implementation or its tamper-detection matrix, and ran no verification. Not searched: third-party audit of the verifier.
Verdict: **link** — no claim changes; cited as the worked example of the signing-time trust boundary.
Artefact: proposed one-line addition to exp 001 *Known limitations*: audit completeness is bounded by the signer's trustworthiness at signing time
Revisit: 2027-05-06 (re-check whether an independent evaluation exists)

### 4. Structural revocation: agents that never hold the credential
Source: https://github.com/agenthof/agenthof · Published: 2026-10-30 (catalogue addition `#121`) · Checked: 2026-11-06 · Grade: B · Maturity: implemented
Claim: An Apache-2.0 Go control plane gates the four doors an agent uses (model, tool/MCP, exec, spawn), injects each credential per call so the agent never possesses it, and writes a hash-chained ledger attributing every action *and refusal* to the human who asked; the project states plainly that it "governs and audits; it does not contain the agent" and that its ledger is "tamper-evident, not tamper-proof".
Why it matters here: It separates two things Agora's T3 ("stop it") currently conflates — **revoking authority** (architectural, immediate, because no credential is held) and **containing a running process** (the operator's sandbox, explicitly out of scope). That is the same split IR 8621 reaches from the incident side, arrived at independently.
Against: Self-reported, with no independent evaluation and no published test of revocation latency. Its own scoping admits the containment half of T3 may fail, so a clean T3 pass cannot be claimed from this design alone. Acceptance is claimed against a "hermetic harness" with stand-ins.
Verdict: **test** — run as the C3-family (gate-first) candidate in exp 001, timed on T3 with the containment step separated out and scored as its own partial.
Artefact: issue to open — *exp 001: C3 variant, credential-injection gate, T3 scored in two steps*
Revisit: 2027-01-06, at first exp 001 run

### 5. Agentic test-case corpus with crosswalks, unvalidated
Source: https://github.com/agentrust-io/awesome-ai-governance/pull/171 · Published: 2026-10-30 · Checked: 2026-11-06 · Grade: C · Maturity: documented
Claim: A CC BY 4.0 Hugging Face dataset of 110 agentic test cases, each with a manual procedure and specified evidence to capture, mapped to OWASP Agentic 2026, NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act and ISO/IEC 42001 themes.
Why it matters here: Exp 001 deliberately excludes multi-step and adversarial conditions; a case corpus is the cheapest route to the stage-2 tests it needs, and the "evidence to capture" column is the part that bears on auditability.
Against: Author-submitted to a curated list, with the author disclosing authorship and no validation, scoring, or inter-rater data. A crosswalk is a mapping claim, not evidence that a case tests what it says it tests. Not searched: independent use of the corpus.
Verdict: **defer** — no capacity this cycle, and it would smuggle an unvalidated instrument into the rubric if adopted before exp 001's first run.
Artefact: none — link only, register entry
Revisit: 2027-02-06, revived when exp 001 moves to adversarial/multi-step cases
