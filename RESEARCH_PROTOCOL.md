# Agora Research Protocol

**Status:** Working protocol · **Version:** 0.1 · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-28

## 1. Frame the question

State the decision or knowledge gap, unit and level of analysis, place, time period, affected groups, and intended use. Separate descriptive, interpretive, causal, normative, and design questions. For proposals, write down the expected mechanism and what observation would count against it.

## 2. Build a traceable search

Record databases, repositories, terms, language, date searched, inclusion/exclusion rules, and access limitations. Search primary materials where feasible (laws, constitutions, institutional rules, decisions, archives, datasets, evaluations) and relevant peer-reviewed or methodologically transparent scholarship. Search for criticism, null findings, failed implementations, and perspectives from groups affected. A search-result snippet is a lead, not verification.

## 3. Appraise sources and claims

For each material source, record author/publisher, title, publication date, URL/DOI/archive, source type, methods/data, context, funding/conflicts when disclosed, limitations, and the exact claims it supports. Distinguish primary evidence, systematic synthesis, scholarly interpretation, advocacy, and commentary. Verify quotations against the source. Track the publication date separately from the date a framework, dataset, or law was last updated.

Use the source-record template. Claims that materially support a conclusion should be traceable to evidence. Do not turn repeated citation into independent corroboration if sources rely on the same underlying evidence.

## 4. Compare institutions in context

Describe the institution's actual rules and practice, not only its label or founders' intentions. Compare mechanisms: who participates; how representation/consent works; how authority is allocated and constrained; how rules are enforced; how disputes and appeals work; how resources and information flow; how exit, voice, and revision operate; and under what ecological, historical, legal, and power conditions. Note selection effects, survivorship bias, translation problems, and limits of cross-cultural categories.

Avoid ranking unlike systems on an unvalidated universal scale. Where comparison is useful, make dimensions explicit and preserve context. Do not infer universal causality from a single case or correlation.

## 5. Keep fact, value, and design separate

Mark statements as **documented**, **empirical finding**, **interpretation**, **normative premise**, **proposal**, or **hypothesis** where ambiguity matters. Identify whose values or rights are prioritized and the trade-offs. Present credible alternatives and counterarguments. Describe who may be harmed, excluded, monitored, burdened, or empowered.

## 6. Assess AI and technology proposals

Map the full workflow and authority chain: people, models, tools, data, integrations, decision rights, external effects, and downstream dependencies. Specify intended and prohibited actions, human review points, escalation, audit evidence, security/privacy risks, failure handling, appeal, rollback, shutdown, and accountable owners. Distinguish a policy document from controls actually enforced at tool and organizational boundaries.

Evaluate against a stated baseline with task-quality, time/cost, error, equity, accessibility, worker/participant experience, privacy/security, and reversibility measures appropriate to the use. Use staged pilots and independent review for consequential use. Model scores or confidence are not independent validators, proof of legitimacy, or sole grounds for permission escalation.

## 7. Treat currency honestly

For claims about current law, standards, products, or active debate, verify at research time from authoritative, dated sources and state the as-of date. Record when sources were last checked and what has not been checked. No process guarantees knowledge "up to today" without a dated search; the repository must expose its freshness limits.

## 8. Write and review

Use the publication-review template before external release. At minimum, a substantive report gets an author/editor review for citation integrity, methods, scope, counterevidence, cultural and distributional coverage, privacy, conflicts, and overclaiming. High-stakes legal, medical, safety, rights, or public-policy outputs require appropriate qualified domain and affected-party review; label their absence plainly.

Correct materially wrong claims promptly, preserve a dated correction record, and keep prior versions discoverable. Agent-generated drafts, summaries, code, or analysis must be disclosed when material and independently checked. No agent may publish or represent an Agora position without human approval.
